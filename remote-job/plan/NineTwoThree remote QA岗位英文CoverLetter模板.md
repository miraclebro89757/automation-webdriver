### **My Analysis and Suggestions**

Thank you for sharing your cover letter. It’s very sincere and passionate, which is great! Your strong alignment with NineTwoThree's values and focus on AI shines through in the letter.

However, from the perspective of a professional cover letter, we can make some key optimizations to make it more professional, confident, and aligned with Western workplace communication styles.

1.  **On Length and Structure:**
    * **The Issue**: Your letter is quite long, spanning seven paragraphs. A standard cover letter should be more concise, typically 3-4 paragraphs, to ensure the hiring manager can quickly grasp your core message.
    * **The Optimization**: We can merge similar content and remove unnecessary details to make the letter more impactful and focused.

2.  **On Content and Tone:**
    * **Issue 1 (Work Culture)**: You mentioned the "challenging overtime culture in China" and a "painful" experience. While this is an honest reflection of your feelings, directly expressing negative sentiments about a past employer or work environment is **highly risky** in a cover letter. It might be interpreted by recruiters as a negative or complaining attitude, or an inability to adapt to demanding situations.
    * **The Optimization**: Shift the focus from "what you are escaping" to "what you are seeking." You can positively state your commitment to efficiency and work-life balance without providing a detailed, negative explanation of your past experiences.

    * **Issue 2 (Spoken English)**: You candidly stated that you feel "less confident" in your spoken English. In a cover letter, **you should never proactively mention your weaknesses**. The purpose of a cover letter is to sell your strengths. If spoken English is a challenge, you should let your strong written communication in the letter speak for itself. Mentioning it only plants a seed of doubt in the recruiter's mind.
    * **The Optimization**: Completely delete this paragraph. Your excellent written English already demonstrates your communication abilities.

3.  **On Highlighting Key Strengths:**
    * **The Issue**: You placed your most crucial AI testing experience in the fourth paragraph, following personal feelings and generic skills. The most compelling information should be front and center.
    * **The Optimization**: We should combine your most unique strengths (AI, LLM testing) with your automation and performance testing experience and place them in the first half of the letter as your core selling point.

4.  **On the Conclusion:**
    * **The Issue**: Mentioning the desire to be the "first Chinese employee" is a kind sentiment but can sound a bit unprofessional in a formal job application.
    * **The Optimization**: Rephrase this to a more general and professional statement, such as "bringing a unique cultural perspective to your diverse team."

* **Shorter and more concise**: Reduced from 7 to 5 paragraphs, with core information placed upfront.
* **More confident tone**: Eliminated all mentions of "painful experiences" and "lack of confidence," focusing solely on your abilities and positive attitude.
* **Highlights core strengths**: The second and third paragraphs are now dedicated to showcasing your most relevant skills: performance testing, automation, Python, and valuable AI/LLM experience.
* **Professional phrasing**: Replaced colloquial or overly emotional language with more formal and professional vocabulary.
* **Clear closing**: Ends with a concise and powerful statement, clearly expressing your eagerness to follow up.

This version presents you as an experienced, highly capable, confident, and professional AI test expert to the hiring manager.
